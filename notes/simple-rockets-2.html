How I became an engineer in Simple Rockets 2.

18 Oct 2026programminggamesciencesandboxrocketsengineer

Simple Rockets 2 is where code first started to make sense to me: not as syntax, but as cause and effect.

ABOUT THE GAME

Simple Rockets 2

A spaceflight sandbox: design rockets, planes and stations part by part, program them, then fly them.

VISIT SIMPLEROCKETS.COM ↗

Game by Jundroo · simplerockets.com. Illustrations here are my own, drawn as inline SVG.

01The aerodynamic rocket

Every rocket starts as a question: why does it wobble, why does it stall? I redesign, fly, read what went wrong, and fix it. That loop of build, test and debug is the same one I use when I write code. One of my best builds is a rocket with good aerodynamics: a clean shape that cuts through the air instead of fighting it.

02The space station

Then I built a huge space station with many docking ports and filled it with astronauts. Docking only works when both sides share the same shape, and that is how I understood interfaces: a contract between two parts that never see each other's insides. Many docks also means many ships can visit at once.

03Programs that fly themselves

My favorite part is programming the embedded chip inside the game, and it is so much fun. My programs run on their own: staging is a sequence, pitch is a feedback loop that reads the angle and corrects it, and deploying the payload is a condition that fires at the right moment. Watching a rocket obey what I wrote made sequence, loop and condition feel real instead of abstract.

Code stopped being abstract when it flew a rocket.

04One of the fastest planes

I also made one of the fastest planes in the game. I strapped a rocket on it, used the tinker panel to keep the weight light, and wrote a small program so the rocket pushes the plane with unlimited fuel. Every gram counts, so I learned to think in constraints: what to keep, what to cut, what to automate.

05VTOL rocket

I built a VTOL rocket too. It rises and lands on its own thrust, so it needs small corrections again and again, which is exactly what a loop does in code.

06From game to code

Looking back, the game was a gentle way into programming. Every idea I met while playing has a twin in code:

StagingSequencestep 1 → step 2 → step 3
PitchFeedback looperror = target - angle
DeployConditionif (altitude >= goal) deploy()
DocksInterfaceconnect(portA, portB)
WeightConstraintif (mass > limit) cut()
Test flightDebuggingfly() → read → fix()

The game never told me this was programming. It just kept asking me to think clearly, test honestly and fix patiently, and that is the habit I carry into every line of code. Want to try? Start at simplerockets.com.
