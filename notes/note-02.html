PERSONAL NOTE 02 / 2026

The moment I stopped watching the chart.

trading / psychology 6 min read personal log

There was a point where checking the chart felt like doing the work. It wasn't.

I used to think more screen time meant more opportunities. More candles, more setups, more chances to catch the move. In reality, I was often just feeding my attention to every little movement.

A setup doesn't become better because I stare at it longer. A loss doesn't become easier because I immediately hunt for the next trade. And a green candle isn't an invitation.

Sometimes the most disciplined thing I can do is close the chart.

That changed how I want to trade. I still study structure, liquidity, entries and risk. But I want the process to survive even when the screen is off. The goal is not to predict every move. The goal is to know my setup, define the invalidation, take the risk I can actually accept, and walk away.

if (setup === 'clear' && riskIsDefined) {
  execute();
} else {
  closeChart();
}

For me, stepping away isn't wasted time. It's part of the system. There is school, prayer, family, exercise, coding, and simply being a person outside the candles. A trading plan that requires me to watch every second is not freedom yet.

I'm still learning this. Some days I break my own rule and come back to the screen too quickly. The difference now is that I notice it — and I can reset before one bad decision turns into a whole session.
